# Step 3: Investor Readiness and Fundraising

## Purpose

After you validate the marketplace and build a basic trusted operation, the next step is to prepare for investment.

Investors do not invest only in ideas. They invest in proof, market opportunity, team ability, and growth potential.

## When to Start This Step

Start investor preparation early, but actively seek investment after you have pilot results.

Recommended before fundraising:

- Working product
- Real sellers
- Real customers
- Real orders
- Basic revenue or commission model
- Clear operational learnings
- Evidence of demand

## Main Objective

Prepare strong investor materials and approach the right investors with proof that BazarAF can become Afghanistan’s trusted online marketplace.

## Investor Story

Your story should be clear:

BazarAF is building a trusted online marketplace for Afghanistan, connecting verified local sellers with customers through Cash on Delivery, local language support, and reliable order operations.

## Problem Statement

Explain the Afghanistan-specific problems:

- Online shopping trust is low.
- Many sellers sell informally through Facebook/Instagram.
- Customers have difficulty finding reliable products online.
- Delivery is not standardized.
- Digital payments are limited.
- Sellers lack modern e-commerce tools.
- There is no dominant trusted national marketplace.

## Solution Statement

BazarAF solves these problems through:

- Verified sellers
- Cash on Delivery
- Product approval
- Customer support
- Local language experience
- Order tracking
- Seller tools
- Gradual delivery partnerships
- Future fulfillment network

Track and present:

- GMV: Gross Merchandise Value
- Revenue
- Number of orders
- Average order value
- Customer acquisition cost
- Repeat purchase rate
- Seller growth
- Product listings
- Cancellation rate
- Delivery success rate
- Monthly active users
- Conversion rate

## Funding Strategy

Do not ask for too much too early.

### Pre-Seed Funding Goal

Raise enough to cover:

- Hosting and infrastructure
- Small team salaries
- Seller onboarding
- Customer support
- Low-cost marketing
- Delivery partnerships
- Legal registration
- Product improvements

### Suggested Use of Funds

- 30% operations and support
- 25% marketing and seller acquisition
- 20% technology and product development
- 15% delivery partnerships
- 10% legal, admin, and emergency reserve

## Types of Investors to Approach

### Afghan Business Owners

Good for:

- Local market knowledge
- Business connections
- Offline credibility

### Afghan Diaspora Investors

Good for:

- Access to foreign capital
- Interest in Afghanistan development
- Strategic mentorship

### Startup Accelerators

Good for:

- Mentorship
- Small funding
- Investor network
- Pitch training

### NGOs and Development Programs

Good for:

- Digital economy support
- SME empowerment
- Women-owned business support
- Youth entrepreneurship funding

### Strategic Partners

Examples:

- Delivery companies
- Telecom companies
- Payment providers
- Large wholesalers
- Retail chains

## Investor Outreach Strategy

### Step 1: Build Target List

Create a list of:

- 50 potential investors
- 20 business owners
- 10 startup programs
- 10 diaspora contacts
- 10 strategic partners

### Step 2: Warm Introductions

Use:

- Friends
- Family business networks
- LinkedIn
- University networks
- Afghan entrepreneur groups
- Startup communities

### Step 3: Send Short Message

Keep the message short.

Example:

We are building BazarAF, a trusted online marketplace for Afghanistan. We have developed the platform over 6 months and are preparing a pilot with verified sellers and Cash on Delivery. We are looking for strategic investors and advisors to help us scale. Can we schedule a short call?

### Step 4: Pitch Meeting

In the meeting:

- Show the live product
- Explain market problem
- Show traction
- Explain business model
- Ask for advice first
- Then discuss investment

## Legal and Company Preparation

Before serious investment, prepare:

- Business registration plan
- Founder agreement
- Equity structure
- Basic accounting system
- Terms and conditions
- Privacy policy
- Seller agreement
- Return policy

## Business Model Options

### Commission

Take percentage from each order.

Example:

- 5% to 15% depending on category

### Featured Listings

Sellers pay to promote products.

### Seller Subscription

Monthly fee for advanced seller tools.

### Delivery Margin

Small margin on delivery fee.

### B2B Orders

Bulk orders for offices, schools, and companies.

## Investor Concerns and Answers

### Concern: Afghanistan market is risky.

Answer:

Start city by city, use COD, verify sellers, keep operations lean.

### Concern: Delivery is difficult.

Answer:

Use delivery partnerships first. Build own logistics only after order volume grows.

### Concern: Customers may not trust online shopping.

Answer:

Use verified sellers, COD, phone support, reviews, and clear return policy.

### Concern: Competitors may enter.

Answer:

Local execution, seller relationships, trust network, and Afghanistan-specific operations are the advantage.

## Success Criteria

Move to Step 4 when:

- Investor conversations are active
- You have funding or strong partnership support
- You can afford controlled growth
- Operations are stable in the first city
- You have enough demand to expand categories or areas

## What Not to Do

Do not:

- Give too much equity too early
- Accept investors with no strategic value
- Raise money without a clear spending plan
- Promise unrealistic growth
- Hide operational problems
- Spend investment mostly on office decoration

## Final Goal of Step 3

Secure investment, strategic partnerships, or grants that allow BazarAF to grow without losing control of costs and quality.
