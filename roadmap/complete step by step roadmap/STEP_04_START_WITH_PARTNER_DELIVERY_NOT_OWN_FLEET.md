# Step 4: Start With Partner Delivery, Not Own Delivery Fleet

## Main Goal

Solve delivery in a low-cost way by using partners and manual coordination before building your own delivery company.

## Why This Step Matters

Delivery is one of the hardest and most expensive parts of e-commerce in Afghanistan. Building your own delivery fleet requires motorcycles, drivers, salaries, uniforms, fuel, management, insurance, cash control, and tracking systems.

You should not do this at the beginning.

## Delivery Strategy for Early Stage

Use a partner-based delivery model.

Possible partners:

- Local courier services
- Taxi drivers
- Motorcycle delivery people
- Rickshaw drivers
- Seller self-delivery
- Independent delivery agents
- Local logistics startups

## Recommended Delivery Model

Start with manual dispatch.

Flow:

1. Customer places order.
2. Admin confirms order by phone.
3. Seller confirms stock.
4. Admin assigns delivery partner.
5. Delivery partner collects product.
6. Delivery partner delivers to customer.
7. Customer pays Cash on Delivery.
8. Cash is recorded.
9. Seller payout is calculated.
10. Order status is updated.

## Delivery Area Strategy

Do not deliver everywhere at first.

Start with selected zones.

Example Kabul zones:

- Central areas first
- High-demand neighborhoods
- Easy access areas
- Areas close to sellers

Avoid early:

- Remote areas
- Unsafe areas
- Areas with unclear addresses
- Areas with high delivery failure

## Cash on Delivery Strategy

COD is important for trust in Afghanistan.

But COD creates risks:

- Customer refuses delivery
- Fake orders
- Cash handling mistakes
- Delivery person delays payment return
- Seller payout disputes

Control COD through:

- Phone confirmation before dispatch
- Order ID for every delivery
- Cash collection sheet
- Daily reconciliation
- Delivery partner agreement
- Customer cancellation tracking

## Delivery Partner Agreement

Create simple written rules.

Include:

- Delivery fee
- Delivery zones
- Expected delivery time
- Cash collection rules
- Failed delivery rules
- Product damage responsibility
- Lost product responsibility
- Payment settlement schedule
- Contact person

## Delivery Fees

Keep fees simple.

Possible models:

- Flat delivery fee by city zone
- Seller pays delivery for promotions
- Customer pays delivery
- Free delivery above certain order value later

Do not offer free delivery everywhere unless you can afford it.

## Delivery Tracking

At first, tracking can be simple.

Use statuses:

- Pending
- Confirmed
- Ready for Pickup
- Picked Up
- Out for Delivery
- Delivered
- Failed
- Returned

Later, build advanced tracking only when order volume requires it.

## Failed Delivery Handling

Failed deliveries will happen.

Reasons:

- Customer phone off
- Wrong address
- Customer changed mind
- Delivery partner delay
- Product not ready

Process:

1. Record reason.
2. Contact customer again.
3. Try one more delivery if reasonable.
4. Return product to seller.
5. Track repeat fake customers.

## Delivery Quality Metrics

Track:

- Delivery success rate
- Average delivery time
- Failed delivery reasons
- Delivery cost per order
- Cash collection accuracy
- Customer complaints
- Partner performance
- Return-to-seller rate

## When to Build Own Delivery

Only consider your own delivery when:

- Daily order volume is high
- Partner delivery is unreliable
- You have investment
- You have enough orders in same zones
- You can manage cash securely
- Delivery cost can be reduced by owning operations

## Mistakes to Avoid

Do not:

- Buy motorcycles early.
- Hire many drivers early.
- Promise same-day delivery everywhere.
- Deliver to all areas immediately.
- Ignore cash reconciliation.
- Use delivery people without basic verification.

## Final Output of This Step

At the end of this step, BazarAF should have a working delivery process using partners, with clear delivery zones, fees, statuses, and cash handling rules.
