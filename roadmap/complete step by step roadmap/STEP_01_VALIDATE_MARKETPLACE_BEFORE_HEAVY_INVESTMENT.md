# Step 1: Validate the Marketplace Before Heavy Investment

## Main Goal

Prove that BazarAF can work in Afghanistan before spending serious money on offices, warehouses, delivery fleets, or large marketing campaigns.

## Why This Step Matters

You have built the project for about 6 months, but the real test is not only whether the software works. The real test is whether Afghan customers will buy from it and whether Afghan sellers will use it.

Before seeking large investment or building expensive operations, you need proof that the marketplace has demand.

## What You Should Do Now

Start with a small controlled pilot instead of a full national launch.

Recommended approach:

- Launch in one city first.
- Start with selected sellers only.
- Use limited product categories.
- Process early orders manually if needed.
- Use partner delivery instead of your own fleet.
- Focus on learning and proving demand.

## Recommended First City

Start in Kabul if possible.

Reasons:

- More buyers
- More sellers
- Better delivery options
- More internet users
- Easier access to shops
- Better investor visibility

## Early Product Categories

Start with categories that are easy to deliver and have regular demand.

Recommended categories:

- Mobile accessories
- Electronics accessories
- Beauty and cosmetics
- Fashion accessories
- Small home products
- Books and stationery
- Computer accessories

Avoid at the beginning:

- Furniture
- Heavy appliances
- Fresh food
- Medicine
- Expensive fragile items
- Products with complex returns

## Seller Target

For the first pilot, do not try to onboard hundreds of sellers.

Target:

- 10 to 30 trusted sellers
- 200 to 500 products

Choose sellers who:

- Have real products
- Have a shop or reliable location
- Respond quickly
- Agree to Cash on Delivery rules
- Accept basic return conditions
- Can provide real product photos

## Customer Target

Start with customers who are easier to reach.

Recommended early customers:

- Friends and family networks
- University students
- Young professionals
- Social media followers
- Local WhatsApp communities
- Customers already buying from Instagram/Facebook sellers

## Operations Strategy

At this stage, manual work is acceptable.

You can manually:

- Approve sellers
- Upload products
- Confirm orders by phone
- Assign delivery
- Update order status
- Record payments
- Collect customer feedback

Use simple tools:

- Google Sheets
- WhatsApp Business
- Phone calls
- Admin dashboard
- Trello or Notion

## Success Metrics

Track:

- Number of sellers contacted
- Number of sellers approved
- Number of products listed
- Number of registered customers
- Number of verified customers
- Number of orders placed
- Number of orders delivered
- Number of cancellations
- Customer complaints
- Repeat customers

## Success Criteria

This step is successful if:

- Customers place real orders.
- Sellers want to continue.
- Deliveries can be completed.
- Customers trust Cash on Delivery.
- You identify the biggest operational problems.
- You collect useful metrics for investors.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Do not:

- Launch in many cities at once.
- Spend heavily on advertising.
- Rent an expensive office.
- Build warehouses.
- Buy delivery vehicles.
- Accept every seller without checking quality.
- Add too many product categories too early.

## Final Output of This Step

At the end of this step, you should have real proof that BazarAF can generate orders in Afghanistan with limited money and controlled operations.
