# Step 7: Focus on Afghanistan-Specific Problems

## Main Goal

Design BazarAF around the real problems of Afghanistan instead of copying foreign marketplaces exactly.

## Why This Step Matters

Afghanistan has unique market conditions. A marketplace that works in the US, Turkey, India, or UAE may not work the same way in Afghanistan.

BazarAF should be built for Afghan customers, Afghan sellers, Afghan delivery challenges, and Afghan trust issues.

## Problem 1: Low Trust in Online Shopping

Many customers may fear:

- Fake products
- Losing money
- Wrong delivery
- No refund
- Unknown sellers

Strategy:

- Cash on Delivery
- Verified sellers
- Phone confirmation
- Customer support
- Return policy
- Real reviews

## Problem 2: Cash Preference

Many customers prefer cash instead of online payments.

Strategy:

- Make COD the default payment method.
- Add digital payments later as optional.
- Train delivery partners on cash handling.
- Track cash carefully.
- Confirm orders before dispatch.

## Problem 3: Address Difficulty

Many addresses may not be structured clearly.

Strategy:

- Use phone confirmation.
- Ask for nearby landmark.
- Allow WhatsApp location sharing.
- Let customers add detailed delivery notes.
- Start with limited delivery zones.

## Problem 4: Delivery Infrastructure Limitations

Delivery may be inconsistent.

Strategy:

- Start city by city.
- Use local delivery partners.
- Track delivery performance.
- Avoid remote areas at first.
- Build delivery zones gradually.

## Problem 5: Limited Digital Payment Adoption

Online payments may not be common.

Strategy:

- Use COD first.
- Add bank/mobile payment later.
- Do not force customers to pay online early.
- Educate customers slowly.

## Problem 6: Language Diversity

Afghanistan has multiple languages.

Strategy:

- Support Dari.
- Support Pashto.
- Support English where useful.
- Use simple wording.
- Make customer support available in local languages.

## Problem 7: Seller Digital Literacy

Many sellers may not know how to use online seller dashboards.

Strategy:

- Provide onboarding help.
- Upload products for early sellers.
- Use WhatsApp for seller communication.
- Create short training videos.
- Keep seller dashboard simple.

## Problem 8: Internet and Device Limitations

Some users may have slow internet or basic phones.

Strategy:

- Keep frontend fast.
- Optimize images.
- Make mobile experience simple.
- Avoid heavy pages.
- Support phone/WhatsApp ordering later if needed.

## Problem 9: Economic Pressure

Customers are price-sensitive.

Strategy:

- Show affordable products.
- Promote discounts honestly.
- Avoid fake discounts.
- Offer delivery fee clarity.
- Highlight value and trust.

## Problem 10: Regional Differences

Customer behavior may differ between Kabul, Herat, Mazar, Kandahar, and other areas.

Strategy:

- Expand city by city.
- Learn each city separately.
- Use local partners.
- Adapt delivery and category strategy per city.

## Product Strategy for Afghanistan

Prioritize:

- Mobile-first design
- COD checkout
- Local language support
- Simple order tracking
- WhatsApp support
- Verified sellers
- Low-data pages
- Clear delivery information

## Operations Strategy for Afghanistan

Use:

- Phone confirmation
- WhatsApp communication
- Manual seller onboarding
- Delivery area restrictions
- Simple return policy
- Local partnerships

## Marketing Strategy for Afghanistan

Use channels people already trust:

- Facebook
- Instagram
- TikTok
- WhatsApp groups
- Local influencers
- University communities
- Seller pages

## Mistakes to Avoid

Do not:

- Copy Amazon/Daraz blindly.
- Force online payments too early.
- Ignore local language needs.
- Assume addresses are simple.
- Launch nationwide without local operations.
- Build complex seller tools before seller education.

## Final Output of This Step

At the end of this step, BazarAF should feel like a marketplace built specifically for Afghanistan, not a foreign marketplace copied into Afghanistan.
